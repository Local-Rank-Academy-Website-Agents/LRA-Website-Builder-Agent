Katy property guide

Property and water-system context in Katy

Katy spans an incorporated city and a much larger suburban market across three counties, many utility districts, and extensive master-planned development.

Older city homes, newer subdivisions, apartments, schools, retail centers, warehouses, pools, irrigation, and large commercial sites create diverse systems. These differences affect the number of meters and zones, pipe routes, access points, operating schedules, and the systems that must be separated before a source is assigned.

Moisture clues that need careful separation

Expansive clay soil, long buried services, multiple MUDs, irrigation, pool plumbing, and fast growth can affect both responsibility and leak location. A current leak should be supported by meter, pressure, moisture, thermal, acoustic, drainage, or water-level evidence that connects the symptom to an active system.

Preparing a Katy property for testing

Provide the exact address and subdivision, utility district if known, meter and valve locations, gate access, and any recent foundation, pool, or landscape work.

Before the visit, note the first symptom, timing, active water use, recent weather, prior repairs, remodels, irrigation cycles, pool operation, appliance use, and whether a building manager or tenant controls any access point.

Private piping, utilities, and later repair

The service boundary can change with the actual provider, meter, MUD, city limit, private site main, tenant agreement, or property-side responsibility. Those details should be verified for the exact Katy address. Detection does not authorize excavation, opening finishes, structural work, remediation, restoration, or utility-side repair.

What property details matter in Katy?+

Why is the exact Katy address necessary?+
