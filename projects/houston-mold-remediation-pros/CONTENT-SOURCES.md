# Content and Policy Sources

The site copy is original. These sources informed service taxonomy, safety framing, and the separation between mold assessment and remediation.

## Texas regulation

- Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, Mold Assessors and Remediators:  
  https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/
- Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, Mold Assessors and Remediators FAQs:  
  https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldfaq.htm

Key use: distinguish assessment from remediation; avoid claiming an unverified license; explain that project-specific requirements and exemptions exist; avoid presenting testing as universally required.

## Mold and moisture guidance

- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Mold Cleanup in Your Home:  
  https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home:  
  https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Mold Remediation in Schools and Commercial Buildings Guide:  
  https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-remediation-schools-and-commercial-buildings-guide-chapter-6
- U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, Should You Have the Air Ducts in Your Home Cleaned?:  
  https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/should-you-have-air-ducts-your-home-cleaned

Key use: center moisture control; describe containment and material decisions without promising a method; include HVAC-specific cautions; distinguish water sources and commercial planning needs.
