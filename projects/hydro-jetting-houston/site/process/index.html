From symptom to completed scope

How Hydro Jetting Service Works

A clear service process connects the observable condition to field testing, an agreed scope, protected work areas, and documented completion.

01

Describe the condition

Record the property type, affected system, timing, current function, active damage, known equipment or pipe details, previous work, and access.

02

Choose the closest service

Use the service catalog to separate diagnosis, cleaning, repair, installation, inspection, safety work, and maintenance.

03

Inspect and test the system

Confirm materials, equipment, access, utilities, safety conditions, and the actual cause before committing to a method.

04

Review the written scope

Define testing, access, materials, permits, shutdowns, cleanup, completion evidence, exclusions, and work by other trades.

05

Verify the finished work

Check operation, flow, temperature, drainage, controls, leak points, cleanup, and any inspection or follow-up documentation appropriate to the service.

Frequently asked questions

Hydro Jetting process questions

What to expect when the condition, scope, and completion criteria are clearly documented.

Why does service begin with symptoms instead of a requested method?+

The same symptom can have several causes. Inspection and testing help avoid work that treats the wrong system or misses a related defect.

What belongs in the written scope?+

The scope should state testing, access, materials, permits, shutdowns, protection, cleanup, completion evidence, exclusions, and how concealed conditions are handled.

When might another trade be required?+

Electrical, gas, structural, roofing, environmental, utility, restoration, or excavation work may require separate coordination depending on the system and findings.

How should completion be verified?+

Verification should match the service: flow testing, temperature and control checks, leak inspection, camera evidence, pressure checks, startup documentation, or required inspection approval.
